Budget-Aware Hybrid Seismic Trace Selection:
Separating Search Eﬀiciency From Reconstruction
Quality
Wu shanzhou
Abstract—Selecting seismic traces by minimizing a geomet-
ric coherence criterion does not necessarily improve missing-
trace reconstruction. This study develops and audits a fixed-
cardinality hybrid search that combines simulated annealing,
stagnation-triggered local exchanges, elite retention, and bud-
get recycling. A physical-coordinate Fourier dictionary and
boundary-aware coverage constraints provide an explicitly
defined selection objective. We separate search quality from
recovery quality through paired evaluation budgets, common-
count trajectory comparisons, a common external holdout,
and recovery selection restricted to observed traces. In a
frozen 20-seed study, the adaptive method achieves lower mean
coherence than a legacy hybrid in all 12 geometry–budget–
density groups; six comparisons remain significant within a 72-
test Holm family. Stable superiority over simulated annealing
alone is not established. Equal-count comparisons show that
recycling primarily prevents unused evaluations rather than
improving the identical trajectory prefix. A supplementary
joint study contains 1080 recovery records but only six clean
structural templates with two noise conditions. Template-
level comparisons do not establish a universal reconstruction
advantage. Across 24 conditional real-data diagnostics, fixed
recovery gives negative hidden-trace signal-to-noise ratio in
17 cases; inner selection reduces this count to one without
establishing high-fidelity recovery. The real file’s sample en-
coding remains unconfirmed. These results support transparent
budget management and end-to-end validation while exposing
the limits of Fourier coherence as a proxy for curvelet recovery.
Index Terms—Seismic acquisition, trace selection, simulated
annealing, mutual coherence, curvelet reconstruction, repro-
ducibility.
I. Introduction
I
RREGULAR seismic acquisition and deliberate trace
reduction create missing observations that must be
reconstructed before subsequent processing. A sampling
design must balance acquisition density, spatial cover-
age, and the structure exploited by a recovery method.
Compressed sensing establishes that undersampling can
be compatible with accurate recovery under appropriate
assumptions on sparsity and the measurement operator
[1], [ 2]. These assumptions do not imply that every
inexpensive geometric score is a suﬀicient predictor of
recovery quality. In particular, optimizing the coherence
of a sampled Fourier dictionary is different from optimiz-
ing the effective operator used by a redundant curvelet
reconstruction algorithm.
Jittered undersampling is an important reference for
this distinction. It combines irregular sampling with con-
trol of large gaps, motivated by reconstruction in localized
transforms [ 3]. Curvelet-based seismic recovery exploits
multiscale, directional structure [ 4], and the fast discrete
curvelet transform (FDCT) provides an eﬀicient numerical
implementation [5]. These foundations motivate coherence
and coverage criteria, but they do not prove that the best
mask under a particular Fourier frequency convention is
also best for a particular signal and iterative solver. Finite
budgets, nonuniform coordinates, event discontinuities,
and amplitude calibration can further separate these
objectives.
Metaheuristic search adds another attribution problem.
Simulated annealing (SA) can accept worsening moves
to explore a discrete space [ 6]; local improvement and
perturbation are established components of iterated local
search [7]. A hybrid may outperform an early-terminating
baseline simply because it uses more of the permitted ob-
jective evaluations. A faster scoring implementation may
likewise improve time-limited results without changing
the search decisions. These effects should be measured
separately before a combination of familiar mechanisms is
described as an algorithmic advance.
This paper studies selection of entire traces from a one-
dimensional candidate set, with an exact retained-trace
count. It does not address unrestricted continuous place-
ment or claim a general two- or three-dimensional acquisi-
tion theory. The method couples an explicit Fourier/point-
spread-function (PSF) objective with optional coverage
penalties and a physical maximum-gap constraint. Its
adaptive controller transfers unused local-search budget
back to global exploration. The principal scientific ques-
tion is whether this improves the geometric search, and
whether a geometric improvement transfers to missing-
trace recovery.
Three aspects define the contribution. First, a budget-
accounted hybrid implementation records candidate eval-
uations, accepted states, elite states, and phase changes,
enabling an equal-count audit of the mechanisms. Sec-
ond, physical-coordinate coherence and exact-cardinality
coverage feasibility are evaluated against independent nu-
merical constructions. Third, a joint protocol separates the
sampling method from recovery selection by holding out
the same external locations across methods and retaining
the traditional mask-complement evaluation as a separate
task. A data audit and explicit negative results delimit
the real-data interpretation.

B. Baselines and Computational Attribution
The legacy hybrid uses a 64-evaluation SA prefix,
including initialization, followed by an index-ordered ±1
greedy search that stops at local stagnation. Its unused
budget is not recycled. SA-only retains the historical
annealing schedule. SA-budget disables local search but
shares the adaptive implementation’s scoring backend and
full-budget temperature schedule. FixedSwitch uses 64
SA candidate evaluations per global phase; NoRecycle
ends at local stagnation; AdjacentOnly restricts the local
neighborhood; NoPerturb disables the extra perturbation.
FixedSwitch is therefore not another name for the legacy
hybrid.
Uniform-grid evaluation uses an FFT, with O(N log N )
scoring and an additional O(N ) scan for coverage.
Physical-coordinate evaluation precomputes O(N 2) com-
plex phase factors, reducing repeated trigonometric work.
Although only m entries contribute per frequency, the
current implementation scans the full mask for each of
N−1 frequencies; its worst-case scoring cost is O(N 2), not
solely O(N m). Cache construction and storage are O(N 2)
and are included in elapsed search time. The feasibility
dynamic program costs at most O(mN 2) time and O(mN )
storage.
Equal-budget limits do not imply equal consumed
budgets. We therefore compare final objectives and, sep-
arately, truncate paired best-so-far trajectories to the
shorter actual evaluation count. No interpolation or re-
peated terminal values are counted as extra evaluations.
This identifies whether an apparent improvement already
exists over the shared prefix or arises only after the
baseline stops.

B. Frozen Search and Joint Protocols
The v4 search study uses N = 128 uniform candidates
and 59 sorted header offsets, with counts (38, 64, 90) and
(20, 30, 40), respectively. Budgets are 200 and 600, with 20
paired seeds per group. Initial masks, counts, and objec-
tives are matched within each group. Six adaptive-versus-
baseline comparisons across two domains, two budgets,
and three densities form one 72-test family. Comparisons
with different coverage weights remain a separate analysis.
The v5 sensitivity study uses B = 300 , ten seeds,
eight method variants, and seven prespecified minimum-
SA/stagnation-window settings: (16, 8), (16, 24), (32, 8),
(32, 12), (32, 24), (64, 8), and (64, 24). Each protocol shares
an initial mask internally, but v5 uses NumPy jitter
initialization while v4 uses the historical C++ initial-
izer. Matching seed labels do not imply matching masks
across versions. A supplementary sequential time-limit
study rotates method order across seeds, applies a 30-
ms high-resolution Windows performance-counter limit,
and includes initialization and cache cost. Its evaluation-
scheduled cooling is discussed separately below.
The joint synthetic study uses arrays of 256 time
samples and 128 candidate traces, with 4-ms temporal and
12.5-m spatial spacing. Three Ricker events have reference
times 0.22, 0.46, and 0.70 s and frequencies 22, 25, and
28 Hz. Three analytic families represent dipping, curved,
and faulted events. Two structure settings use dip slopes
35/120 µs/m, curvature velocities 2600/1800 m/s, or fault
shifts 25/70 ms, as applicable. Each clean template is
evaluated with additive noise standard deviation 0.01 and
0.05 using frozen seeds. These are analytic event fields,
not wave-equation simulations. The stored target includes
that noise, so ( 11) measures reconstruction of the hidden
noisy traces rather than denoising against a clean target.
For a common external test, fix zero-based indices
H = {4, 12, . . . ,124}, containing 16 locations. Select
m = 38 , 64, 90 traces from the remaining 112 candidates,
while retaining the full 128-location Fourier dictionary.
The nominal retained fractions use denominator 128; they
correspond to 33.9%, 57.1%, and 80.4% of eligible candi-
dates. No waveform in H enters search, recovery, scaling,
or inner selection. All unobserved columns are physically
zeroed before invoking native recovery. Jitter, SA-only,
legacy hybrid, SA-budget, and Adaptive are compared
with three mask seeds. A second design selects on the full
candidate set and evaluates each mask’s own complement.
It preserves the original acquisition question, but its
missing locations and diﬀiculty differ across methods.
There are 1080 joint records: 12 structure–noise condi-
tions, three densities, three mask seeds, five methods, and
two evaluation designs. Ninety geometric search masks
are reused across signal conditions. Fixed and selected
recovery are both stored for the common- H cases. These
counts are process records, not 1080 independent subsur-
face realizations.
C. Statistical Units and Real-Data Assumptions
For search, seeds are paired computational replicates
on a fixed geometry. For joint recovery, average the three
mask seeds within a condition and then the two noise
levels within a clean structural template. This gives six
analysis units, with three event families and two structure
settings. Bootstrap intervals use 10000 resamples with
seed 54127. Paired signed-rank statistics use exact sign
enumeration with tied midranks and report rank-biserial
effects; interpretation assumes an appropriate symmetric
paired-difference model. Holm correction [ 8] is applied
within the stated comparison family. All intervals are
marginal exploratory intervals, not simultaneous cover-
age statements or evidence about a randomly sampled
geological population.
The six-template aggregation was introduced after in-
spection of an initial 12-condition analysis to correct
dependence. Both analyses are retained, and this timing
precludes presenting the correction as a wholly prospective
confirmatory design. The 24 template-level comparisons
comprise four Adaptive contrasts, three counts, and two
recovery modes. Frequency sensitivities and geometry–
recovery correlations are descriptive and never used to
select weights against the external test.
The sole real file contains 59 traces with 250 samples.
Its little-endian headers consistently encode 8000 µs and
sample format code 1, which declares IBM floating point
under SEG-Y [ 9]. The historical reader instead interprets
the payload as little-endian IEEE. Independent byte
auditing supports the header layout and exact file size but
does not prove that payload interpretation. Six alternative
byte/format interpretations produce finite samples. Text-
header and provider-document claims also conflict with
trace-header timing and geometry. Five CDPs have 8,
15, 15, 14, and 7 traces, and offset sorting changes
CDP at every neighboring pair. Coordinate differences
equal 100 times the reported offsets, with no confirmed
scalar or coordinate reference system. Physical-coordinate
experiments therefore use header offsets conditionally, not
as established calibrated acquisition geometry.
The v5 real diagnostics use original numerical ampli-
tudes, no spatial interpolation, no fitted filtering, and no
global normalization before holdout. Four windows are
preset: 250 samples at 8 ms over 0–1.992 s; 128 at 8 ms over
0–1.016 s; 499 at 4 ms over 0–1.992 s; and 256 at 4 ms over
0–1.020 s. Each uses two common geometric splits at each
of 20, 30, and 40 retained traces. Upsampling does not add

V. Results
A. Search Improvement and Mechanism Attribution
In v4, Adaptive has lower mean final coherence than the
legacy hybrid in all 12 geometry–budget–density groups;
six of these contrasts survive the 72-test Holm family.
No corrected contrast establishes stable superiority over
SA-only. Fig. 1 shows the 600-evaluation groups, including
uncertainty across paired computational seeds rather than
across independent survey geometries.
For uniform m = 38 , B = 600 , the paired Adaptive-
minus-hybrid difference is−0.015055, with 95% bootstrap
interval [−0.019027,−0.011239], rank-biserial effect −1,
and Holm-adjusted p = 0 .0001373. The corresponding
Adaptive-minus-SA-only difference is −0.000301, with in-
terval [−0.003289, 0.002413] and effect 0.0667. The sign
convention is lower-is-better. A small favorable mean is
not suﬀicient to claim a reliable algorithmic advantage.
The v5 study produces no significant contrast in its
42-test family; the smallest adjusted p is 0.08203125.
With ten nonzero pairs, the smallest possible two-sided
exact sign probability is 2/210, and even multiplying
this by 42 exceeds 0.05 for the first Holm rejection.
The design therefore has insuﬀicient resolution for that
family, irrespective of an apparently large descriptive
effect. It cannot establish the absence of useful switching
or neighborhood effects.
The equal-count analysis is more directly diagnostic.
Adaptive and NoRecycle are identical until the latter ter-
minates; all six group mean differences over their common
prefixes are exactly zero. Their final differences conse-
quently include additional use of the remaining evalua-
tions. Fig. 2 also shows that this attribution does not imply
all other hybrids have identical prefixes. Independent
universal benefits from switching, mixed neighborhoods,
or extra perturbation remain unestablished. NoPerturb
sometimes has a better descriptive mean and sometimes
a worse one; it was not retrospectively promoted to the
main method.
Under a 30-ms limit, mean measured search durations
range from approximately 30.017 to 30.040 ms, with
about 518–633 evaluations. Adaptive has favorable mean
coherence in that setup. However, B = 100000 remains the
cooling reference, so none of these short runs completes
its evaluation-based annealing schedule. Equal wall-clock
limits do not imply matched cooling progress. These
measurements are conditional timing diagnostics, not a
universal speed claim. Shared-cache savings and search-
policy effects must also be distinguished.

VI. Reproducibility, Limitations, and Implications
Independent checks distinguish implementation fidelity
from scientific suitability. Explicit NumPy dictionaries
and small-mask enumeration verify coherence, coverage,
and hard feasibility in 588 cases. Dense coordinate-
projection references verify two native cases. Twenty
FDCT parameter/size combinations in each of Release
and Debug have maximum relative inverse-transform er-
ror below 2.30× 10−15. These results establish numer-
ical agreement over tested cases; they do not guaran-
tee missing-trace accuracy. A scale-aware observation-
consistency check repairs an input-unit failure without
changing the archived search/recovery conclusions.

Several limitations govern interpretation. First, the real
source’s header structure is supported, but the sample
encoding, physical units, grouping, and coordinate scaling
remain unresolved. Source-matched export metadata and
known numerical samples from the provider are needed.
Second, six prescribed analytic templates and one condi-
tional real source do not establish cross-survey generaliza-
tion. Additional masks or noise settings cannot substitute
for independent geology. Third, the studies compare a
constrained set of search and recovery baselines; modern
signal-adaptive or learned methods are not benchmarked.
No state-of-the-art performance claim follows.
Fourth, exact small-sample testing is coarse. The 42-
test ten-seed search family and the 24-test six-template
recovery family cannot support broad confirmatory con-
clusions. Dependence was corrected transparently after
initial analysis, not concealed. Fifth, the current time-limit
test confounds incomplete cooling with phase behavior,
and the implementation has only been fully checked in
its recorded Windows/VS2008/Qt4 environment. Sixth,
the recovery objective is pointwise error on hidden noisy
traces. Phase, amplitude, moveout, and downstream imag-
ing fidelity require separate measures and independently
justified data.
The practical implication is to retain the simplest
supported mechanisms. Budget recycling prevents a docu-
mented form of premature termination; the evidence does
not require presenting every switch or perturbation as
independently beneficial. Fourier coherence remains a use-
ful reproducible geometric descriptor, but coverage, signal
structure, solver configuration, and acquisition semantics

must accompany it. A future signal-dependent objective

SNR would not resolve the present limitations.
VII. Conclusion
A fixed-cardinality, budget-aware hybrid trace selector
was implemented and evaluated with physical Fourier
coherence and explicit spatial coverage. It improves the
legacy hybrid’s final mean geometric objective in the
frozen comparisons, while stable superiority over SA-
only remains unsupported. Identical Adaptive–NoRecycle
prefixes show that continued use of the allowed evaluations
is the clearest mechanism-level explanation. Common ex-
ternal holdouts and observed-only recovery selection reveal
that geometric improvement and reconstruction improve-
ment are separate claims. The six-template joint study and
conditional real-data diagnostics do not establish universal
recovery gains or high-fidelity real-data reconstruction.
The resulting contribution is a reproducible, budget-
accounted selection and validation workflow with explicit
negative evidence and clearly delimited data assumptions.
